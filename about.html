about

sixteen years of community work, and a growing cookbook problem

a little context

I'm a community management and marketing leader. I've been lucky enough to lead teams at reddit, Depop, WeWork, Nike, Teal, Commsor, Midjourney, and Nextdoor. Since July 2026 I've run my own practice, which is mostly community platform strategy, neighborhood health measurement, AI readiness work, and archival research.

At reddit I was the first community manager and the fifth employee, and later the General Manager. That job taught me most of what I know: that communities are not audiences, that volunteers will out-work any team you can hire, and that the interesting problems are almost never the ones on the roadmap.

I'm also an amateur history nerd with a specific obsession with early 1900s church cookbooks and other communal projects of yore. Which is less of a detour than it sounds. A community cookbook is a distributed, volunteer-run, user-generated content project with a moderation policy and a fundraising model. People have been doing this work for a very long time. Most of them didn't call it that.

I write three newsletters about all of it: Assorted Wormholes, Chapel Hill Rear View, and Covered Dish.
