When you work in community, you work for two companies.

The 1st one has a Delaware address & a dental plan. It holds all-hands meetings where someone always lies about the roadmap.

The 2nd one doesn’t exist on an org chart. It lives in 3 AM posts & Discord channels. It’s the cathedral your longtime members build from feature requests that read like prayers.

The 1st company can be valued. The 2nd one is invaluable. We tell ourselves we can serve both. Your job is to translate between the two. if you do your job right, you’ll betray both.

Morning standup: User sentiment is flat
Translation: They’re building gallows in the forum
Community post: We hear your concerns about the update
Translation: The adults made a decision. I’m so sorry

There’s a user who’s been here 12 years. Unpaid. Unbreakable. Writes better docs than product marketing.

Tomorrow her favorite feature gets deprecated.
Tomorrow you write the announcement.
Tomorrow she’ll understand you were never entirely on her side.
And she’ll forgive you anyway. Which is worse.

We tell ourselves we stay for practical reasons. Salary. Benefits. Resume.

The truth is stranger. Getting paid to care about actual humans feels like resistance. Like smuggling something real through the checkpoint.

It’s Sandra in Sydney’s garden photos. Marcus teaching strangers in your forums for 5 years, never asking for anything. The feeling, however compromised, that you’re still touching something human inside the machine.

Whether that’s true or just another story is a Sunday evening question.

The community thinks we work for them.
The company thinks we work for them.
We work for the space between.
we get to live in both worlds.

Most corporate jobs insulate you from consequences. Executives make decisions from orbit. The machine is designed to separate you from the people out there. This job won’t let you forget them.

We’re in the room when they kill the feature. In the Discord when Sandra finds out. We carry cause & consequence in one body.

That’s the burden. Here’s the gift:
Tragic wisdom. Executives aren’t evil, they’re constrained. Users aren’t irrational, they’re grieving.

9 AM: Board meeting prep. Defendable metrics.
Noon: Love letters disguised as bug reports.
Both educations. Both yours.

The split isn’t a wound. It’s a doorway.
Like those Victorian spirit photographers who caught ghosts through double exposure. Except both exposures are real. Both ghosts alive.

We tell ourselves we’re building bridges. Maybe.

Or maybe we’re standing in the river, one foot on each bank, pretending the water isn’t rising.

Tomorrow the navbar will change. Tomorrow we’ll explain it in two languages. And if we’re lucky, both companies survive a bit longer because of that translation.

When you work in community, you work for two companies.

One pays your salary. The other gives you a reason to cash the check.

Forever split. Forever whole.

I wouldn’t know how to be one thing anymore. I’m not sure I ever was.
