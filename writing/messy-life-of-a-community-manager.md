# The messy life of a community manager

By Erik Martin. Published 2025-05-09. Canonical URL: https://hueypriest.com/writing/messy-life-of-a-community-manager/

Don’t work in community. Seriously.

I never meant to become a community manager. Most of us didn’t.

We fell into it sideways, like walking through the wrong door at a party and suddenly finding ourselves responsible for babysitting someone’s forgotten & drunk cousin.

The job is a messy tapestry of contradictions.

You'll build fences designed to be climbed, write rules you know damn well will be broken.

You'll yearn to belong but remain eternally apart.

And if you grip too tight, you'll strangle what makes it beautiful. Let go completely, and you’ll watch it turn feral. Either way, you’ll lose what matters nine times out of ten (at best).

But oh, the success. The success is the even crueler teacher.

That's when the real gangly paradoxes emerge, when old-timers speak in code purposefully pushing newcomers to wander lost. when cherished memories become walls, when you must break precious things to save them.

Communities eventually need controlled burns: enough fire to clear the undergrowth, not enough to consume the forest.

But you don’t get to control the flames. At best, you create conditions where it might burn without consuming everything. Sometimes your eyebrows will get singed. Just part of the deal.

Your victories will be invisible. Arguments that never escalate. Newcomers who never feel small. Veterans who never turn bitter.

The metrics will tell stories of engagement and abandonment, time spent and attention fractured. The numbers will be precise and meaningless, spreadsheet logic imposed on human ritual.

Eventually, some executive will look at the budget and wonder why they're paying for something that seems to run itself. You'll try to explain without sounding defensive. You'll fail.

You’ll tell yourself stories that somehow the right words might bridge those unbridgeable gaps. You’ll tell yourself we're mounting a quiet resistance against disconnection.

You will tell yourself that community is salvation to the central paradox of our time: We have never been more connected, yet never more alone.

But you know deep down that you helped cement that paradox.

And yet, if you’re naive enough to hold all these contradicting truths in your hands without crushing them—well then, this might be the only job in the world for you: unpredictable, turbulent, and occasionally sublime.

You can still make another choice but not really.
Someone needs to make sure the drunk cousin is going to be ok.

At least that’s the story I tell myself
